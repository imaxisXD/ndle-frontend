A bio link is set once and clicked for months. Followers tap it long after you've forgotten where it points, so it's the link most likely to end up on a page that's moved, sold out or gone.

## Why bio links go stale

- **The page changes after you post.** A launch ends, a product sells out, a sign-up form closes.
- **Nobody checks.** A bio link doesn't show an error to you, only to the follower who taps it.
- **Old posts keep sending people.** "Link in bio" in a post from last spring still points at today's link.

## What ndle does for a bio link

- **It's checked every 30 minutes.** ndle checks the page behind every short link, and flags it on your Monitoring page when it returns a 404, times out or fails its certificate check.
- **You see who taps it.** Clicks by day, country, device, referrer and UTM campaign, on the free plan.
- **It can be yours.** The free plan includes one custom domain, so your bio can read `go.yourbrand.com`.
- **No ad page.** A tap goes straight to your page.
- **A QR code comes with it,** for posters, packaging and business cards that point to the same place.

## Five links, one bio

Instagram lets you add up to five links to your bio, though only the first shows on your profile; the rest sit behind it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/18/instagram-takes-on-linktree-and-others-with-support-for-up-to-5-links-in-bio/)). In March 2026 Meta also began testing clickable links in post captions for Meta Verified subscribers ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/meta-is-testing-clickable-links-in-instagram-captions-for-verified-subscribers-184555406.html)).

Every one of those links can be an ndle link. Tag each with `utm_source=instagram` and `utm_medium=social` using the free [UTM builder](/tools/utm-builder), and Instagram traffic is easy to spot in ndle and in Google Analytics.

## What ndle isn't

ndle isn't a link-in-bio page builder. It doesn't make a page of buttons like Linktree does. It makes the links themselves: short, counted, and checked. Use ndle links directly in your bio, or on whatever bio page you already have.
